Sir Alex Ferguson

The greatest manager in football history, Sir Alex Ferguson led Manchester United from 1986 to 2013, winning 38 major trophies including 13 Premier League titles, five FA Cups, and two UEFA Champions League crowns.

Roy Keane

The ultimate captain and midfield general, Roy Keane drove Manchester United's dominance across 12 years at Old Trafford, winning seven Premier League titles and inspiring the historic 1999 Treble campaign.

David Beckham

A jewel of the famous Class of '92, David Beckham dazzled Old Trafford with his pinpoint crossing, trademark free-kicks, and tireless work ethic, winning six Premier League titles and the 1999 UEFA Champions League.

Paul Scholes

Revered by peers as one of the finest midfielders of his generation, Paul Scholes spent his entire career at Manchester United, making 718 appearances, scoring thunderous long-range goals, and winning 11 Premier League titles.

Eric Cantona

Known simply as "King Eric" to the Old Trafford faithful, Eric Cantona arrived in 1992 with his iconic upturned collar and swagger, serving as the catalyst for Manchester United's 1990s dynasty with four league titles in five seasons.

Gary Neville

A fiercely loyal one-club man and Class of '92 graduate, Gary Neville made 602 appearances at right-back for Manchester United, captaining the side and capturing eight Premier League titles and two UEFA Champions League trophies.

Cristiano Ronaldo

Arriving as a teenage prodigy in 2003, Cristiano Ronaldo transformed into the world's best player at Old Trafford, winning three consecutive Premier League titles, the 2008 UEFA Champions League, and the 2008 Ballon d'Or.

Wayne Rooney

Manchester United's all-time leading goalscorer with 253 goals in 559 appearances, Wayne Rooney combined relentless passion with world-class brilliance to win every major club honour available at Old Trafford.

Ruud van Nistelrooy

One of the most lethal strikers ever to grace the Premier League, Ruud van Nistelrooy scored an astonishing 150 goals in just 219 appearances for Manchester United between 2001 and 2006.

Edwin van der Sar

Calm, commanding, and composed, Edwin van der Sar anchored United's defence to four Premier League titles, set a world-record 1,311-minute clean-sheet streak, and made the decisive penalty save in the 2008 Champions League final.

Peter Schmeichel

The "Great Dane" redefined goalkeeping in the 1990s with his towering presence and acrobatic saves, captaining Manchester United in the 1999 UEFA Champions League final to complete the historic Treble.

Ole Gunnar Solskjær

Immortalized for scoring the injury-time winner against Bayern Munich in the 1999 Champions League final, the "Baby-Faced Assassin" scored 126 goals for United before later returning to manage the club.

Andy Cole

A prolific goalscorer who formed a legendary strike partnership with Dwight Yorke, Andy Cole netted 121 goals in 275 appearances for Manchester United, playing a pivotal role in five Premier League titles and the 1999 Treble.

Ryan Giggs

Manchester United's all-time appearance record holder with 963 matches across 24 seasons, Ryan Giggs is the most decorated player in English football history, winning 13 Premier League titles and two Champions League trophies.

Rio Ferdinand

The epitome of a modern ball-playing centre-back, Rio Ferdinand combined elegance and athleticism across 455 appearances for Manchester United, winning six Premier League titles and the 2008 UEFA Champions League.

Nemanja Vidić

A fearless warrior at the heart of defence alongside Rio Ferdinand, club captain Nemanja Vidić won five Premier League titles, the 2008 Champions League, and two Premier League Player of the Season awards.

Patrice Evra

Adored for his infectious passion and attacking flair down the left flank, Patrice Evra made 379 appearances for Manchester United, winning five Premier League titles and the 2008 UEFA Champions League.
